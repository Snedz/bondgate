BondGate

An onchain approval gate for AI agents — $WIF bonds settled by a deterministic, recomputable policy verdict on Robinhood Chain.

PRE-DEPLOY DEMO PAGE — the BondGate contract is not yet deployed. Everything below that is labelled a test run happened locally, offline. No mainnet address, transaction, or receipt exists yet, and none is implied.

What it is

An AI agent that wants to take a real action first posts a $WIF bond against the hash of the action it intends to take. The payload is hash-bound at queue time. A deterministic policy gate — an 8-class policy table, no LLM in the decision path — evaluates the action class and re-verifies the payload hash at execution time.

  • Approve → the action executes, the bond is released.
  • Reject → the bond is forfeited to a pool.
  • Unsafe (payload differs from its bound hash) → the bond is locked for a seven-day evidence window, then forfeitable.
  • A bond the operator never settles becomes reclaimable by the agent after the settle window — no bond can be stranded by silence.

The verdict is the product. A BondGate verdict is a pure function of the hash-bound payload and a published policy table — anyone can recompute it offline and prove a wrong settlement from the receipt chain. BondGate sells verifiable operation, not trustless operation: the operator runs the gate, but cannot plausibly lie about what the gate decided.

Why $WIF: during this event, $WIF's utility in BondGate is the bond-escrow unit plus the event's own community-vote burn (votes burn $WIF — a sink the chain already runs), and the forfeit pool is a misbehavior premium, not a yield engine.

Not the only bonded entry, and it doesn't claim to be: PACT, another entry in this event, bonds workers at 50% of job price with hash-bound terms and a human resolver. BondGate gates permission to act rather than payment for hired work, and its verdict — unlike a resolver's ruling — can be recomputed bit for bit. Bond sizes here are runner parameters: the full demo runs end-to-end on a ~$10 float.
